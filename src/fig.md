# Figure Programme

## 3. The programme

### Figure 1 — The 1980s body-conscious silhouette
**Placement:** §2, P6, beside the Steele and Arnold sentences.
**Must show:** the dominant Paris or international silhouette of the early 1980s on a body: padded shoulders, defined waist, emphatic structure.
**Why it is needed:** P6 asserts that the convention was an active aesthetic programme, and §6's inversion rests on the Paris garments being opposite to an imposed silhouette. The reader currently has no image of what was imposed. Without it, half the inversion is asserted rather than shown.
**Source route:** a dated editorial or runway photograph from 1982–83. The V&A, the Museum at FIT and the Fashion History Timeline all hold or reproduce period images. A catalogued garment of the period (a Mugler, Montana or Saint Laurent suit) is the safer option if an image with clear provenance is hard to secure.
**Status:** not yet identified. The only figure in this set with no source in hand.

### Figure 2 — Kawakubo jumper
**Placement:** §4, P11.
**Status: confirmed.**
Victoria and Albert Museum, record O73390, accession T.167 to C-1985, given by Rei Kawakubo.
**Caption text, from the record:** jumper of black hand-knitted wool, designed by Rei Kawakubo, Tokyo, 1982, for Comme des Garçons; decorated with holes placed at random; the body knitted in stocking stitch with the holes placed at random to create a slashed effect.
**One thing to state:** the jumper is one element of an ensemble comprising jumper, skirt and boots, each separately recorded. Say so rather than implying it stood alone.

### Figure 3 — Yamamoto ensemble
**Placement:** §4, P11.
**Status: confirmed.**
The Kyoto Costume Institute, "Jacket, Dress, Pants", Spring/Summer 1983; gift of Mr Hiroshi Tanaka (jacket and pants) and Ms Sumiyo Koyama (dress). Published through KCI's Google Arts & Culture collection.
**Caption text, from the record:** white cotton plain weave; cut-work. Deliberately created holes in oversized clothes produced by straight cutting; the holes form conventionalised flower patterns; the edge of each hole is elaborately finished.
**Why this caption matters most:** the finishing detail is the material evidence that the deprivation reading was a misreading. Putting it in the caption is what lets P12 and P17 argue rather than describe.

### Figure 4 — Chikanobu triptych
**Placement:** §5, P14. Referred back to in §6, P17, which is normal practice and needs no second figure.
**Status: confirmed.**
Yōshū Chikanobu, *Illustration of the Ladies' Charity Bazaar at the Rokumeikan*, 1887. Polychrome woodblock print (nishiki-e), vertical ōban triptych. Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, 2000.495a–c.
**Two cautions for the caption.** Take dimensions and the credit line from the MFA record itself, not from the circulating scan. And the print depicts a charity bazaar of 1887, not the November 1886 ball Loti attended: the caption should not let the reader assume it illustrates his evening.

### Figure 5 — The collections worn
**Placement:** §6, P17.
**Must show:** garments from the 1982–83 collections on bodies, ideally several looks together.
**Why it is needed, and this is the most important point in this document:** §6 argues at the level of the relation between garment and body, volume displacing the outline and an interval opened between cloth and skin. **A jumper photographed flat on a mount cannot show that.** Figures 2 and 3 give construction; they do not give body-relation. Meanwhile Figure 4 shows bodies wearing. As the set currently stands, the Rokumeikan side of the inversion is visible and the Paris side is not, which inverts the argument's own asymmetry and weakens exactly the claim §6 exists to make.
**Source route:** runway or editorial photography of the autumn-winter 1982–83 or Spring/Summer 1983 showings. The FIDM Museum's Michel Arnaud archive holds Comme des Garçons runway photography of this period and publishes it with provenance. Period editorial reproduced in a catalogue with a figure credit is the alternative.
**Status:** not yet identified.
