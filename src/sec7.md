# §7 — the verdict and its implications

*Submitted text runs continuously, without headings or paragraph labels, as in §4 to §6.*

---

The answer the evidence supports is neither reproduction nor challenge but a particular relation between them. Reproduction was structural rather than merely prevalent, since the operations ran independently of what the garments did, and no object could have disarmed a reading that was not attending to it. Challenge was correspondingly displaced. The collections constituted a genuine alternative proposition, yet a proposition cannot become counter-pressure within a discourse indifferent to it, and the pressure the discourse did register came instead from the people it had described: from Kawakubo's account of her own strategy, from the critics who refused the explanation by origin, and from Akutagawa's rewriting of the evening Loti had described. That pressure was in turn shaped by the frame it contested, since Koda discarded national origin only to reach for Zen in its place, and Akutagawa's rewriting carried its own imperial ambivalence.

Beyond the two receptions examined above, the structure has proved durable. The grouping of designers by national origin persists in the terms by which the work continues to be exhibited, and a vocabulary that explains practice by reference to a national sensibility remains available whenever work from outside the Western field requires accounting for. What the comparison suggests is that the remedy lies neither in better objects nor in more sympathetic criticism, since sympathy reproduced the operations as faithfully as hostility did, and the critics who took the work most seriously performed the denial of coevalness while refusing the marking of origin. It lies instead in attending to what a given reading is attending to, a question answerable only when a reception is set against the object it claims to describe.
