## Why they are not duplicates

B1 is a **theory-led** chapter: its subject is philosophical deconstruction and its implications for fashion, with Japanese designers treated as part of a broader deconstructive field that also includes Margiela and Belgian designers. [ualresearchonline.arts.ac](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/20478/)

C15 is a **design-history/reception-led** chapter: its subject is the Japanese designers’ challenge to Western fashion canons at their Paris debut, and it explicitly rejects the reduction of their work to mere “Japaneseness” or foreign otherness. [dokumen](https://dokumen.pub/fashion-wise-1nbsped-9781848881600-9789004371965.html)

The overlap is thematic—not textual duplication. Both discuss Miyake, Kawakubo, and Yamamoto, and both connect their practice to deconstruction, but C15 develops the historical claim about a “fashion revolution,” whereas B1 develops the Derridean conceptual apparatus. [ualresearchonline.arts.ac](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/20478/)
